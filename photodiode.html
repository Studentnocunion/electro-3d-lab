Photodiode Working Principle

A photodiode is a semiconductor device that converts light energy into electrical current. It works mainly in reverse bias.

1. Light Absorption

When light photons strike the semiconductor junction, they transfer energy to electrons and create electron-hole pairs.

2. Charge Carrier Movement

The electric field in the depletion region separates the generated electrons and holes, producing a photocurrent.

3. Photocurrent Generation

The reverse current increases with the intensity of incident light. Greater light intensity generally produces greater photocurrent.

4. Dark Current

Even without incident light, a small reverse current may flow. This is called dark current.

Important: A photodiode is commonly operated in reverse bias for light detection.

Photodiode Construction and Terminals

A photodiode is made using a semiconductor PN junction. It has a light-sensitive region and two electrical terminals.

1. P-Type Semiconductor

The P-type region contains holes as majority charge carriers.

2. N-Type Semiconductor

The N-type region contains electrons as majority charge carriers.

3. Depletion Region

The region near the PN junction where free charge carriers are depleted.

4. Light-Receiving Window

Allows incident light to reach the photosensitive semiconductor region.

Photodiode Terminals

Anode (+)

Connected to the P-type region. It is the positive terminal in the usual reverse-bias connection.

Cathode (-)

Connected to the N-type region. It is connected to the positive supply in reverse-bias operation.

Important: In reverse bias, the cathode is connected to positive voltage and the anode to negative voltage.

Photodiode Operating Modes

1. Photoconductive Mode

In this mode, the photodiode operates under reverse bias.

  • Cathode is connected to positive supply.
  • Anode is connected to negative supply.
  • Depletion region becomes wider.
  • Photocurrent increases with light intensity.
  • Provides fast response.

Common use: Optical communication and light detection circuits.

2. Photovoltaic Mode

In this mode, no external bias voltage is applied to the photodiode.

  • Operates at zero external bias.
  • Light generates electron-hole pairs.
  • Produces a voltage across the device.
  • Can operate with low noise.
  • Used for light-to-electrical energy conversion.

Common use: Light measurement and solar energy applications.

Applications of Photodiode

Photodiodes are widely used in electronic systems to detect and measure light.

1. Optical Communication

Converts incoming optical signals into electrical signals in fibre-optic systems.

2. Light Sensors

Detects light intensity in automatic lighting and brightness-control systems.

3. Smoke Detectors

Detects changes in light caused by smoke particles inside a detector.

4. Remote Control Receivers

Detects infrared light signals from remote-control devices.

5. Medical Instruments

Used in optical measurement equipment such as pulse oximeters.

6. Barcode Scanners

Detects reflected light patterns while scanning barcodes.

Important Photodiode Formulas

1. Total Photodiode Current

I = Idark + Iphoto

Depending on current direction and sign convention, the photocurrent contribution may be written with a minus sign.

2. Responsivity

R = Iphoto / Popt

R = Responsivity (A/W)

3. Electrical Power

P = V × I

Electrical power = Voltage × Current

4. Photocurrent Relationship

Iphoto ∝ Popt

Photocurrent is approximately proportional to incident optical power under fixed operating conditions.

Symbols and Units

  • I = Total current (A)
  • Idark = Dark current (A)
  • Iphoto = Photocurrent (A)
  • R = Responsivity (A/W)
  • Popt = Incident optical power (W)
  • V = Voltage (V)

Photodiode Quick Revision Notes

1. Definition

A photodiode is a light-sensitive semiconductor device that converts light into electrical current.

2. Construction

It consists of a PN junction with a light-receiving window.

3. Common Operating Mode

Photoconductive mode uses reverse bias.

4. Charge Carriers

Incident photons generate electron-hole pairs in the semiconductor.

5. Photocurrent

Photocurrent generally increases as incident light intensity increases.

6. Dark Current

A small current can flow even when no light falls on the photodiode.

7. Photovoltaic Mode

In this mode, the photodiode operates without external bias.

8. Responsivity

It is the ratio of photocurrent to incident optical power, measured in A/W.

9. Terminals

A photodiode has two terminals: anode and cathode.

10. Applications

Optical communication, light sensors, smoke detectors and infrared receivers.

Photodiode Technical Glossary

1. Photodiode

A semiconductor device that detects light and produces electrical current.

2. Photocurrent

Electrical current generated due to incident light.

3. Dark Current

Small current flowing through a photodiode in the absence of incident light.

4. Responsivity

Ratio of photocurrent to incident optical power, expressed in A/W.

5. Depletion Region

Region around a PN junction depleted of mobile charge carriers.

6. Reverse Bias

Biasing condition in which the cathode is at a higher potential than the anode.

7. Electron-Hole Pair

A free electron and a corresponding hole generated when a photon transfers sufficient energy.

8. Photovoltaic Effect

Generation of voltage in a semiconductor due to incident light.

9. Optical Power

Light energy transferred per unit time, measured in watts.

10. Quantum Efficiency

Ratio of collected charge carriers to incident photons, under a specified measurement condition.
