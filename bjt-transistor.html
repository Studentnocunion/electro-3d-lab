Working Principle

An NPN transistor is a three-terminal semiconductor device consisting of two N-type regions separated by a thin P-type region.

A small base current controls a larger collector current when the transistor operates in the forward-active region.

Conventional current flows from Collector to Emitter in normal active operation.

RC — Collector Resistor

Understanding Electron Flow

In an NPN transistor operating in forward-active mode, electrons move from the N-type emitter, through the thin P-type base, towards the N-type collector.

Conventional current flows in the opposite direction: from collector to emitter.

Only a small fraction of injected electrons recombine in the base, producing the base current.

PN Junctions in an NPN Transistor

Emitter–Base Junction

EB Junction

Forward biased in the forward-active operating mode. It allows electrons to enter the base region.

Collector–Base Junction

CB Junction

Reverse biased in the forward-active operating mode. It helps collect electrons into the collector.

Forward Active Mode: EB = Forward Bias | CB = Reverse Bias

NPN vs PNP Transistor

NPN Transistor

Structure: N-P-N

Majority carriers: Electrons

Conventional current: Collector to Emitter

Base voltage: Higher than emitter for forward-active operation

PNP Transistor

Structure: P-N-P

Majority carriers: Holes

Conventional current: Emitter to Collector

Base voltage: Lower than emitter for forward-active operation

Important BJT Formulas

Collector Current

IC = β × IB

Collector current equals current gain multiplied by base current.

Emitter Current

IE = IC + IB

Emitter current is the sum of collector and base currents.

Current Gain

β = IC / IB

Common-emitter DC current gain.

Collector–Emitter Voltage

VCE = VC - VE

Voltage difference between collector and emitter.

Note: Use consistent units when calculating transistor currents.

NPN BJT – Quick Revision Notes

1. Definition

A Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) is a three-terminal semiconductor device used for amplification and switching.

2. Three Terminals

Emitter (E), Base (B) and Collector (C).

3. Structure

An NPN transistor consists of two N-type regions separated by a thin P-type base.

4. Charge Carriers

Electrons are the majority charge carriers in an NPN transistor.

5. Forward Active Mode

Emitter–Base junction is forward biased and Collector–Base junction is reverse biased.

6. Current Relationship

IE = IC + IB

7. Current Gain

β = IC / IB

8. Main Applications

Amplifiers, electronic switches, oscillators and digital circuits.

BJT Technical Glossary

BJT

Bipolar Junction Transistor – a three-terminal semiconductor device.

Doping

Adding impurities to a semiconductor to change its conductivity.

Depletion Region

Region near a PN junction depleted of mobile charge carriers.

Biasing

Applying DC voltage to establish a transistor operating condition.

Current Gain (β)

Ratio of collector current to base current in common-emitter configuration.

Saturation

Operating condition in which both transistor junctions are forward biased.
