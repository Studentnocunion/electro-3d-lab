JFET Working Principle

1. Basic Concept

A Junction Field Effect Transistor (JFET) is a voltage-controlled semiconductor device that controls current using an electric field.

2. Semiconductor Channel

Current flows through a semiconductor channel between the Drain and Source terminals.

3. Gate Control

The Gate-to-Source voltage controls the width of the depletion regions and therefore the channel conductivity.

4. Current Control

Changing the Gate-to-Source voltage changes the Drain current.

5. Reverse Bias

In normal operation, the Gate-to-Channel PN junction is reverse biased, resulting in very small gate current.

6. Pinch-Off

As the reverse bias increases, the channel narrows. At pinch-off, the Drain current approaches saturation for a given Gate voltage.

JFET Construction and Terminals

1. Semiconductor Channel

An N-channel JFET uses an N-type semiconductor channel through which electrons flow from Source to Drain.

2. Gate Region

Two P-type regions form PN junctions with the N-type channel. These regions are connected to the Gate terminal.

3. Source (S)

The Source terminal supplies majority charge carriers into the channel.

4. Drain (D)

The Drain terminal collects majority charge carriers from the channel during normal operation.

5. Gate (G)

The Gate controls channel width through reverse bias. Its current is normally very small.

6. Majority Charge Carriers

Electrons are the majority carriers in an N-channel JFET. A P-channel JFET uses holes.

Note: This laboratory demonstrates an N-channel JFET. A P-channel JFET has the opposite semiconductor polarities and voltage polarities.

JFET Operating Modes

1. Ohmic (Linear) Region

The JFET behaves approximately like a voltage-controlled resistor. Drain current increases with Drain-to-Source voltage.

2. Pinch-Off (Saturation) Region

The channel becomes constricted near the Drain. Drain current becomes approximately constant for a fixed Gate-to-Source voltage.

3. Cutoff Region

When the reverse Gate-to-Source voltage reaches the cutoff value, the channel closes and Drain current becomes nearly zero.

Important: Pinch-off does not mean the JFET is switched off. In the saturation region, current can still flow. Cutoff is the condition where the Drain current is nearly zero.

JFET Applications

1. Amplifiers

JFETs amplify weak electrical signals in electronic circuits.

2. Buffer Circuits

High input impedance makes JFETs useful as buffer amplifiers between circuit stages.

3. Low-Noise Circuits

JFETs are used in sensitive audio and measurement circuits where low noise is important.

4. Analog Switches

JFETs can control analog signals by switching the channel between conducting and cutoff conditions.

5. Constant-Current Sources

JFET circuits can provide approximately constant current over a range of operating voltages.

6. Impedance Matching

JFETs help connect circuit stages with different impedance levels while reducing signal loading.

Important JFET Formulas

1. Shockley Equation

ID = IDSS × (1 − VGS / VGS(off))²

Calculates Drain current in the saturation region for an N-channel JFET.

2. Drain Current at VGS = 0

ID = IDSS

At zero Gate-to-Source voltage, Drain current is approximately IDSS in saturation.

3. Drain-to-Source Voltage

VDS = VD − VS

Voltage difference between the Drain and Source terminals.

4. Gate-to-Source Voltage

VGS = VG − VS

Controls the channel width and Drain current.

5. Drain Power

PD = VDS × ID

Power dissipated by the JFET, using the stated voltage and current sign convention.

Symbol meanings: ID = Drain current, IDSS = Drain current at VGS = 0 in saturation, VGS = Gate-to-Source voltage, and VGS(off) = Gate cutoff voltage. For an N-channel JFET, VGS and VGS(off) are normally negative. The Shockley equation is an idealized model.

JFET Quick Revision Notes

  • Full Form: Junction Field Effect Transistor.
  • Device Type: Unipolar, voltage-controlled semiconductor device.
  • Terminals: Gate, Drain and Source.
  • Channel Types: N-channel and P-channel.
  • Gate Junction: Normally reverse biased during operation.
  • Input Impedance: High.
  • Gate Current: Very small.
  • Current Control: Gate-to-Source voltage controls Drain current.
  • Pinch-Off: Drain current enters the saturation region for a given gate voltage.
  • Cutoff: Drain current becomes nearly zero.
  • Important Equation: Shockley equation.
  • Applications: Amplifiers, buffers, analog switches and constant-current sources.

JFET Technical Glossary

JFET

Junction Field Effect Transistor, a voltage-controlled semiconductor device.

Channel

The semiconductor path through which current flows between Drain and Source.

Gate

The control terminal that regulates channel conductivity.

Depletion Region

A region around a PN junction with very few mobile charge carriers.

Pinch-Off

The condition where the channel becomes constricted near the Drain and current approaches saturation.

Cutoff Voltage

The Gate-to-Source voltage at which Drain current becomes nearly zero.

IDSS

The Drain current at zero Gate-to-Source voltage in the saturation region.

Reverse Bias

A bias condition that widens the depletion region of a PN junction.
