Zener Diode Working Principle

1. Definition

A Zener diode is a specially designed PN junction diode that operates in the reverse breakdown region to maintain an approximately constant voltage.

2. Forward Bias

In forward bias, the Zener diode behaves similarly to an ordinary silicon diode and conducts current after its forward voltage is reached.

3. Reverse Bias

In reverse bias, only a small leakage current flows until the reverse voltage reaches the Zener breakdown voltage.

4. Zener Breakdown

When the reverse voltage reaches the breakdown voltage, the diode conducts significant reverse current while maintaining an approximately constant voltage across it.

5. Voltage Regulation

A Zener diode can maintain a stable output voltage when connected in reverse bias with a suitable series current-limiting resistor.

Important Note

A series resistor is essential in a practical Zener regulator to limit current and protect the diode from damage.

Zener Diode Terminals and Characteristics

1. Anode

The P-type region of the Zener diode. It is the positive terminal in forward bias.

2. Cathode

The N-type region of the Zener diode. It is the negative terminal in forward bias.

3. Zener Voltage (VZ)

The specified reverse breakdown voltage at which the Zener diode begins operating in its breakdown region.

4. Dynamic Resistance (rZ)

The small change in Zener voltage divided by the corresponding change in Zener current around an operating point.

5. Breakdown Region

The reverse-bias operating region where the voltage remains approximately constant while current changes within the permitted range.

6. Power Rating

The maximum power the Zener diode can safely dissipate under specified conditions.

Zener Diode Voltage Regulator Circuit

A Zener diode is connected in reverse bias across the load to maintain a nearly constant output voltage.

Working

  • Vin provides the input DC voltage.
  • Rs limits the current flowing through the circuit.
  • The Zener diode is connected in reverse bias.
  • RL represents the load resistance.
  • Vout remains approximately equal to VZ when the Zener operates within its regulation range.

Zener Diode V-I Characteristics

The V-I characteristics describe how current changes with voltage in forward and reverse bias.

1. Forward Bias Region

When the anode is positive relative to the cathode, the Zener diode behaves like an ordinary silicon diode. Significant forward current flows after the forward voltage reaches approximately 0.7 V.

2. Reverse Leakage Region

When reverse biased, only a very small leakage current flows before the breakdown region is reached.

3. Reverse Breakdown Region

When the reverse voltage reaches the Zener breakdown voltage (Vz), the reverse current increases rapidly. The voltage across the diode remains approximately constant over its specified operating-current range.

4. Important Terms

  • Vz: Zener breakdown voltage.
  • Iz: Reverse current through the Zener diode.
  • Iz(min): Minimum current needed for specified regulation.
  • Iz(max): Maximum permitted operating current.
  • Rz: Dynamic resistance in the breakdown region.

Important: Always use a suitable series resistor to limit current and keep the Zener diode within its rated power and current limits.

Applications of Zener Diode

Zener diodes are widely used in electronic circuits where a stable reference or controlled voltage is required.

1. Voltage Regulation

Maintains an approximately constant output voltage despite suitable changes in input voltage or load current.

2. Voltage Reference

Provides a stable reference voltage for measurement and control circuits.

3. Overvoltage Protection

Helps protect sensitive electronic components by limiting voltage when connected in a suitable protection circuit.

4. Waveform Clipping

Used in clipping circuits to limit signal voltage to selected levels.

5. Meter Protection

Can protect sensitive measuring instruments from excessive voltage.

6. Switching Circuits

Used in some switching and threshold-detection circuits.

Zener Diode vs Ordinary PN Junction Diode

Parameter Zener Diode Ordinary PN Diode
Main Purpose Voltage regulation Rectification
Reverse Bias Designed to operate in breakdown Normally blocks reverse current
Doping Heavily doped Generally less heavily doped
Depletion Region Narrow Generally wider
Breakdown Designed for controlled breakdown Usually avoided during normal operation
Applications Voltage regulation and protection Rectifiers and switching circuits

Remember: A Zener diode is designed to operate safely in its specified reverse-breakdown region when current and power are properly limited.

Important Zener Diode Formulas

1. Series Current

Is = (Vin − Vz) / Rs

2. Load Current

IL = Vz / RL

3. Zener Current

Iz = Is − IL

4. Zener Power

Pz = Vz × Iz

5. Series Resistance

Rs = (Vin − Vz) / (Iz + IL)

Symbols

  • Vin = Input voltage (V)
  • Vz = Zener voltage (V)
  • Rs = Series resistance (Ω)
  • RL = Load resistance (Ω)
  • Is = Series current (A)
  • IL = Load current (A)
  • Iz = Zener current (A)
  • Pz = Zener power (W)

Important: These formulas assume the Zener diode is regulating at approximately Vz. Always check minimum and maximum Zener current, resistor power, and diode power ratings.

Zener Diode – Quick Revision Notes

Definition

A Zener diode is a specially designed PN junction diode that operates in reverse breakdown to maintain an approximately constant voltage.

Important Points

  • It is a heavily doped PN junction diode.
  • It has a narrow depletion region.
  • It is mainly used in reverse bias.
  • Its breakdown voltage is called Zener voltage (Vz).
  • It maintains approximately constant voltage in its regulation region.
  • A series resistor limits the current.
  • It can be used in voltage regulators and protection circuits.

Remember These Formulas

  • Is = (Vin − Vz) / Rs
  • IL = Vz / RL
  • Iz = Is − IL
  • Pz = Vz × Iz

Exam Tip

The Zener diode is normally operated in reverse bias in the breakdown region for voltage regulation.

Zener Diode – Technical Glossary

  • PN Junction: The boundary formed between P-type and N-type semiconductor materials.
  • Reverse Bias: A condition in which the P-side is connected to negative and the N-side to positive supply.
  • Breakdown Voltage: The reverse voltage at which current increases rapidly.
  • Zener Voltage (Vz): The approximate voltage maintained across a regulating Zener diode.
  • Depletion Region: The region around a PN junction depleted of mobile charge carriers.
  • Leakage Current: The small reverse current flowing before breakdown.
  • Dynamic Resistance: The small-signal resistance of the diode in its breakdown region.
  • Voltage Regulation: Maintaining a nearly constant output voltage.
  • Series Resistor: A resistor used to limit current through the Zener diode.
  • Power Rating: The maximum power the Zener diode can safely dissipate under specified conditions.
