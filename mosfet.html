NMOS Working Principle

An NMOS (N-channel Metal Oxide Semiconductor) transistor is a voltage-controlled device.

How does it work?

  • VGS = 0: No conducting channel is formed in the normal enhancement-mode NMOS.
  • VGS above threshold voltage: An electron inversion channel forms beneath the gate oxide.
  • VDS applied: Electrons flow from Source to Drain.
  • Gate control: Increasing VGS above threshold generally increases channel conductivity.

Important Terminals

Gate (G): Controls the channel.

Drain (D): Collects electrons.

Source (S): Supplies electrons.

Body (B): Semiconductor substrate connection.

NMOS Quick Revision Notes

1. Definition

An N-channel MOSFET is a voltage-controlled semiconductor device in which electrons are the majority charge carriers.

2. Four Terminals

  • Gate (G): Controls the channel.
  • Drain (D): Current enters or leaves through this terminal, depending on the chosen current convention.
  • Source (S): Provides carriers to the channel.
  • Body (B): Semiconductor substrate.

3. Operating Regions

  • Cut-off: VGS ≤ VTH
  • Linear: VGS > VTH and VDS < VGS − VTH
  • Saturation: VGS > VTH and VDS ≥ VGS − VTH

4. Important Terms

  • VGS: Gate-to-source voltage.
  • VDS: Drain-to-source voltage.
  • VTH: Threshold voltage.
  • ID: Drain current.

5. Main Characteristics

  • High input impedance.
  • Voltage-controlled operation.
  • Low gate current in normal operation.
  • Widely used in digital and analog circuits.

Important MOSFET Formulas

1. Gate-to-Source Voltage

VGS = VG − VS

2. Drain-to-Source Voltage

VDS = VD − VS

3. Overdrive Voltage

VOV = VGS − VTH

4. Drain Current in Cut-off

ID ≈ 0, when VGS ≤ VTH (ideal model)

5. Drain Current in Linear Region

ID = K [2(VGS − VTH)VDS − VDS²]

6. Drain Current in Saturation Region

ID = K(VGS − VTH)²

7. Transconductance in Saturation

gm = 2K(VGS − VTH)

Note: These current equations use the ideal long-channel square-law model, where K is the process transconductance parameter in this convention. Channel-length modulation is ignored.

MOSFET Technical Glossary

1. MOSFET

Metal Oxide Semiconductor Field Effect Transistor. A voltage-controlled semiconductor device.

2. Threshold Voltage (VTH)

The minimum gate-to-source voltage required to form a conducting inversion channel in an enhancement NMOS.

3. Inversion Channel

A conducting region formed beneath the gate oxide when sufficient gate voltage is applied.

4. Gate Oxide

An insulating layer separating the gate electrode from the semiconductor body.

5. Drain Current (ID)

The current flowing through the MOSFET channel between drain and source.

6. Transconductance (gm)

The rate of change of drain current with respect to gate-to-source voltage at a specified operating point.

7. Pinch-off

The condition near the drain associated with the onset of saturation in a long-channel NMOS.

8. Body Effect

The change in threshold voltage due to a change in source-to-body voltage.

9. Channel Length

The effective distance between source and drain beneath the gate.

10. High Input Impedance

A characteristic of MOSFETs resulting from the insulated gate structure.

Applications of MOSFET

1. Electronic Switching

MOSFETs are widely used as electronic switches in digital circuits and power control systems.

2. Amplifiers

MOSFETs amplify electrical signals in audio, communication and electronic circuits.

3. CMOS Technology

NMOS and PMOS transistors work together in CMOS circuits used in processors and digital ICs.

4. Power Supplies

Power MOSFETs are used in switching regulators, DC-DC converters and power management circuits.

5. Motor Control

MOSFETs control the speed and power of DC motors in electronic drive systems.

6. VLSI Circuits

MOSFETs are the fundamental building blocks of modern integrated circuits and microprocessors.

7. Battery Management

MOSFETs are used for battery protection, charging and electronic power switching.

NMOS vs PMOS Comparison

Parameter NMOS PMOS
Structure N-channel P-channel
Majority Carriers Electrons Holes
Substrate P-type N-type
Channel Formation Positive gate voltage relative to source Negative gate voltage relative to source
Carrier Mobility Generally higher Generally lower
Switching Speed Generally faster Generally slower
Common Application Low-side switching High-side switching

Important Point

NMOS and PMOS transistors are combined in CMOS technology to create low-power digital circuits.
